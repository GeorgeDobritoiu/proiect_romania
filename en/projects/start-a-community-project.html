Start a community project

A 30-day plan: need, team, budget, pilot and results.

George Dobritoiu · Proiect România

Original practical guide by Proiect România. A proposed pilot model, ready to adapt locally.

Start with a verified need

Choose a small, observable issue: no reading club, limited activities for older residents or an uncared-for shared space. Speak to 5–10 affected people. Identify existing services and possible partners.

Fill in a one-page brief

  • Need: who is affected, where and what evidence supports it?
  • 30-day outcome: what small change can you verify?
  • Participants: realistic capacity and invitation method.
  • Activity: venue, date, duration and access arrangements.
  • Owners: decision maker, activity coordinator and spending record keeper.
  • Resources: people, venue, materials, cash and in-kind contributions.
  • Main risk: what could stop the pilot and what is the fallback?

A 30-day plan

  1. Days 1–7: speak with residents, write a one-sentence problem and select a pilot.
  2. Days 8–14: assemble three organisers, confirm venue arrangements and set the budget.
  3. Days 15–21: invite participants, prepare materials and check logistics and accessibility.
  4. Days 22–30: run the pilot, collect feedback and share aggregate results and spending.

Illustrative workshop budget

Assume 12 participants, a free venue and two volunteers. Materials: RON 120; printing: RON 40; transport: RON 80; contingency: RON 60. Total: RON 300. These are planning assumptions, not verified prices. Obtain local quotes and record in-kind contributions separately.

Four measures

  • Attendance divided by places offered.
  • Participants completing the activity divided by attendance.
  • Actual spending divided by attendance; do not calculate this if nobody attends.
  • Feedback responses and requests for a repeat session.

Work transparently

Agree who approves spending and keep receipts. Collect only necessary contact details and explain their purpose and retention period. For children's activities, work with a school or organisation able to manage supervision and participation procedures. Check requirements for the venue and activity before launching.

Decide after the pilot

Continue when interest and resources are confirmed; adapt when participants identify barriers; stop if the need is unsubstantiated. Report what you tried, learned and need next.
